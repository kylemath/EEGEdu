We have prepared an exhaustive Point-by-Point Response document (`paper1_eegedu/reviews/response_to_reviewers.md`) that addresses each checklist item and all individual reviewer comments in full detail, quoting exact revised passages, formulas, and repository code. Below, we summarize the primary enhancements incorporated into the revised manuscript.

First, regarding psychometric reporting standards and frequency-domain parameterization, we addressed the crucial distinction raised by Dr. Pontifex and the Action Editor between cross-frequency spectral profile consistency and temporal epoch stability. In Section 3.3, we refined our description of Cronbach's alpha across the five 1 Hz alpha bins (8–12 Hz; alpha = 0.67 during Eyes Closed and alpha = 0.77 during Eyes Open) and the odd-versus-even bin correlation ($r_{\text{SB}} = 0.91$) to explicitly characterize inter-bin spectral profile consistency rather than temporal measurement reliability across time. We explain that this high inter-bin coherence reflects both physiological co-modulation and deterministic Dirichlet-kernel spectral leakage from untapered rectangular DFT windows, and we document why Cronbach's alpha is slightly lower during Eyes Closed: during visual fixation, power follows a smooth, monotonic aperiodic ramp with uniform covariance, whereas eye closure concentrates oscillatory power into individualized, participant-specific alpha peaks. Complementing this cross-frequency metric, we evaluated true temporal epoch split-half reliability across the 27 consecutive 1.0-second Fourier epochs in our archived multi-epoch dataset (`eeg_spectra.xlsx`), establishing near-perfect temporal stability across channels and frequencies ($r_{\text{SB}} = 0.9973$, with $r_{\text{SB}} = 0.9972$ on posterior-temporal derivations and $r_{\text{SB}} = 0.9559$ across the alpha band; Section 3.2).

Second, we performed an exhaustive audit of the digital signal-processing pipeline in `@neurosity/pipes`, `fili`, and `dsp.js`. In Section 2.2, we clarified that passing `order: 2` to `fili`'s cascade calculator instantiates two cascaded second-order biquad sections per cutoff, generating a causal fourth-order Butterworth high-pass filter ($-24\text{ dB/octave}$ roll-off) in series with a causal fourth-order Butterworth low-pass filter ($-24\text{ dB/octave}$ roll-off), forming an 8-pole bandpass cascade overall. We documented how the trailing-window truncation behavior in `@neurosity/pipes` (`safeSamples.slice(safeSamples.length - bins)`) governs effective Fourier apertures and explains why slider adjustments above 256 samples introduce buffer latency without altering frequency resolution unless bins are coupled to duration. We clarified interface slider behavior in `PageSwitcher.js`, explaining why filter sliders are locked in default classroom views of Modules 3 and 11 to preserve standardized timing while remaining accessible for pedagogical demonstrations of filter settling times and phase distortion. Furthermore, we harmonized our reporting of canonical frequency bands, contrasting `@neurosity/pipes`'s inclusive floating-point bounds with the strictly disjoint integer partitions evaluated in our offline analyses.

Third, we introduced an explicit orienting framework at the opening of Section 3 that categorizes our evaluation into three transparent tiers: multi-participant classroom cohort benchmarks ($N = 59\text{--}83$), single-session empirical demonstrations ($N = 27$ sequential epochs), and synthetic Monte Carlo forward models parameterized to Muse noise floors (Modules 10 and 11). In Section 3.1 and Figure 2B, we explicitly explained that Figure 2B applies full-wave QRS envelope demodulation to the bandpass-filtered waveform from Module 2 to reconstruct the cardiac fundamental ($47.6\text{ BPM}$) and its harmonic series observed live in Module 3. In Section 3.2, Section 3.3, and Table 1, we defined our decibel metrics as $10\log_{10}$ amplitude levels ($\text{dB}_{\text{amp}}$), noting that conventional power decibels ($20\log_{10}\hat{A}$) are exactly twice the tabulated numbers, leaving test statistics, $p$-values, and effect sizes invariant. In `generate_figures.py`, we corrected the analytical power spectral density normalization in Figure 5A, anchored the theoretical square-root-of-$N$ signal-to-noise ratio curve in Figure 5B to the analytical single-trial signal-to-noise ratio, and clearly labeled both synthetic panels. In addition, we built an automated drop-in empirical data loader in `generate_figures.py` accompanied by a standalone recording guide (`TONIGHT_DATA_COLLECTION_PROTOCOL.md`) that allows the user or instructor to collect live Muse data in 10 to 15 minutes, automatically swapping synthetic forward models for empirical recordings upon file detection.

Fourth, drawing upon the wireless hardware and timing analysis from Prof. Debener, we expanded Section 2.2 and Section 3.4 to formalize the mechanics of Bluetooth Low Energy packet framing and arrival timestamping. We detailed how the Muse packs 12 unsigned 12-bit samples per GATT notification ($T_{\text{packet}} = 46.875\text{ ms}$ at 21.33 Hz), how synchronous JavaScript unrolling assigns identical `Date.now()` timestamps across 12-sample bursts, and why offline event-related potential analyses must index time by sample row count relative to trigger markers. Crucially, we incorporated the analytical low-pass transfer function of packet framing and BLE latency jitter on ensemble-averaged event-related potentials, demonstrating mathematically why WebBluetooth acquisition preserves over 90% of slow endogenous components such as the P300 while attenuating rapid sensory potentials. In Module 10, we documented how phase-locked timestamp accumulation eliminates cumulative frequency drift at 11 Hz despite 60 Hz display refresh quantization.

Fifth, to align our machine-learning benchmarks directly with `ml5.KNNClassifier`'s browser implementation, we reported unit-$L_2$ normalized five-fold `GroupKFold` cross-validation results in Section 3.4 and Figure 5C alongside feature standardization. Unit-$L_2$ normalization achieved 76.9% out-of-sample accuracy and an ROC-AUC of 0.78 on alpha-prominence features and 67.9% accuracy and an ROC-AUC of 0.72 on full 60-bin bilateral spectra, closely mirroring feature standardization while faithfully reflecting the browser's internal cosine-similarity classifier mechanics. We also explicitly distinguished this offline cross-subject binary classification benchmark from the live within-subject three-class calibration setup (Eyes Closed, Eyes Open, and Blinking) utilized in Module 12.

Sixth, we deepened the biophysical explanation of our Lead-I electrocardiogram bridge in Section 3.1. We detailed how pinching only the forehead electrodes leaves the right-ear Driven Right Leg contact floating in air, explaining why pre-epoch 20 Hz low-pass filtering is vital to suppress mains hum and how touching a second finger to the ear tab stabilizes the baseline. We elucidated the three compounding mechanisms behind Module 3's downward baseline shift (zero-padding step discontinuities on un-demeaned rectangular windows, biphasic QRS harmonic halving, and transform normalization scaling), demonstrating that these additive biases cancel out in within-subject posture contrasts ($\Delta\text{HR}$). Furthermore, we supplemented the row-aligned intersection test with Welch two-sample $t$-tests comparing the full independent cohorts ($N = 67$ vs. $N = 76$, $p = 0.8455$; symmetric $N = 75$ vs. $N = 76$, $p = 0.8640$) and an ID-realigned cohort from `heart_spectra_group.xlsx` ($N = 58, p = 0.6979$), verifying cross-module concordance independently of spreadsheet indexing.

Seventh, incorporating the neurophysiological insights from Dr. Cannard and Dr. Popov, we detailed the contrasting biophysics of ultra-short anterior forehead derivations (`AF7/8--FPz`, ~3–4 cm) versus long fronto-mastoid derivations (`TP9/10--FPz`, ~14–16 cm). In Section 3.3, we reported that eye closure induces opposite rotations of the non-oscillatory aperiodic background ($\chi_A$) across scalp regions: flattening posterior-temporal spectra ($\chi_A = 1.07 \to 0.99, p = 0.0061$) as low-frequency blinks cease, while steepening anterior-frontal spectra ($\chi_A = 0.75 \to 0.93, p = 1.17 \times 10^{-4}$) as high-frequency frontalis and corrugator muscle tension relaxes. We quantified Individual Alpha Frequency center-of-gravity ($9.87\text{ Hz}$ Closed vs. $9.84\text{ Hz}$ Open) and difference-spectrum peak frequency ($9.87\text{ Hz}$), and we explained why young-adult alpha reactivity spanning 9 to 15 Hz renders our flanking-detrended prominence contrasts conservative lower bounds.

Eighth, addressing the pedagogical error taxonomy highlighted by Dr. Segawa, we formalized the two distinct novice error modes in Module 2: Stage 1 gross spreadsheet formula and noise-threshold errors in `heart_spectra_1.xlsx` (11 rows blanked during pre-screening), and Stage 2 missed R-wave peak errors within `alpha_single.xlsx` (8 rows in 31.7–38.9 BPM removed by the 40 BPM filter and retained at 100% in our symmetric sensitivity check), contrasting both with Module 3's automated 100% retention. We clarified that compiling `alpha_single.xlsx` shifted student IDs by two rows at ID 150 between Module 2 and subsequent columns, and we explained that sample reduction in Module 9 resulted from Google Sheets tab-switching copy-paste attrition across four separate sheets rather than hardware interruption.

Ninth, we expanded Section 2.1 and the Discussion with practical guidance for multi-headset classroom logistics, describing how staggered 30-second row-by-row pairing prevents discovery collisions across the three Bluetooth advertising channels while active streams operate across 37 frequency-hopping data channels. We detailed multi-operator rotation workflows, alcohol-free sanitization procedures to prevent silicone elastomer degradation, battery management across consecutive lab periods, and cross-platform compatibility between `Bluefy` mobile visualization and desktop CSV logging.

Tenth, we eliminated all hardcoded string literals in `generate_figures.py`, routing every statistical value through dynamic numerical formatters and exporting complete macro definitions to `stats_macros.tex` and `stats_summary.json`. We populated all missing non-parametric Wilcoxon signed-rank $p$-values in Table 1, reported test statistics and effect sizes for the 22 Hz frontal EMG trough and single-bin 10 Hz contrasts in Section 3.3, and enriched our bibliography with foundational citations in ERP filter distortion (Tanner, Morgan-Short, & Luck, 2015), psychometric reliability (Clayson & Miller, 2017; Pontifex et al., 2010), automated Individual Alpha Frequency quantification (Corcoran et al., 2018), and mobile EEG validation (Debener et al., 2012).
