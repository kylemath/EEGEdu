# Peer Review Report — *Psychophysiology* (Methodological Developments)

**Manuscript Title:** EEGEdu: A Zero-Install, Reactive WebBluetooth Platform for Human Electrophysiology, Time–Frequency Signal Processing, and Brain–Computer Interfaces  
**Authors:** Keyfer P. Mathewson, Kory W. Mathewson, and Kyle E. Mathewson  
**Reviewer:** Dr. Cedric Cannard (Institute of Noetic Sciences / CERCO; `ccannard@noetic.org`)  
**Date:** November 9, 2026  

---

## Overall Assessment & Compliments

In this Methodological Developments manuscript, Mathewson and colleagues present **EEGEdu** (`https://eegedu.com`), an open-source, zero-install Progressive Web Application (PWA) that streams 256 Hz wireless electrophysiology from InteraXon Muse headbands directly into Chromium browsers via the W3C WebBluetooth API and processes the data in real time using functional reactive streams (`muse-js`, `RxJS`, and `@neurosity/pipes`). The platform is structured around a 12-module curriculum spanning dry-electrode contact quality, Lead-I electrocardiography (ECG) via a forehead-reference finger pinch, multi-channel artifact taxonomy, Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) linear amplitude spectra, canonical frequency band decomposition, closed-loop `p5.js` neurofeedback, rolling spectrograms, steady-state visual evoked potentials (SSVEPs), oddball event-related potentials (ERPs), and in-browser $k$-nearest neighbors (KNN) brain–computer interface (BCI) classification. The authors validate the platform across empirical undergraduate laboratory recordings ($N = 86$ roster IDs; $N = 67$ complete four-condition spectral datasets in Module 9).

As someone who has spent considerable time validating the InteraXon Muse hardware for spectral power, individual alpha frequency (IAF), and frontal alpha asymmetry against research-grade systems (Cannard et al., 2021) and developing open-source EEG signal-processing pipelines, I found this manuscript both technically refreshing and methodologically insightful. Several features deserve special commendation:

1. **Zero-Install Architecture & Transparency:** Anyone who has tried to run `BlueMuse`, `muselsl`, Lab Streaming Layer (LSL), and Python/MATLAB scripts across 50 student laptops with mixed Windows, macOS, and ChromeOS configurations knows how fragile native BLE streaming can be. Moving the entire GATT unpacking, causal IIR Butterworth filtering (`fili`), rectangular epoching, FFT (`dsp.js`), and stimulus generation into the client-side browser sandbox is a major practical achievement for scalable classroom instruction and mobile/field psychophysiology.
2. **The Lead-I ECG Time-to-Frequency Bridge (Modules 2 & 3):** Using the `AF7` versus `FPz` finger pinch to record a transthoracic Lead-I ECG ($V_{\text{LA}} - V_{\text{RA}}$) and contrasting a 2–20 Hz passband (which attenuates the sub-1 Hz cardiac fundamental $f_0 = 0.793\text{ Hz} = 47.6\text{ BPM}$ while preserving the $2f_0$ and $3f_0$ QRS harmonics) against a zero-padded 0.01–20 Hz passband is a brilliant pedagogical demonstration of filter cutoffs, non-sinusoidal impulse harmonics, and Fourier zero-padding interpolation.
3. **Candid Exposition of the `FPz` Reference Dipole and the Module 6 vs. Module 9 Contrast:** Rather than hiding the quirks of consumer dry-electrode EEG, the authors turn the Muse's mid-forehead reference (`FPz`) and the "failed" unguided 10-second Module 6 classroom exercise ($t(82) = -2.82, p = 0.0060$, where apparent 8–12 Hz amplitude was *higher* in Eyes Open than Eyes Closed) into a centerpiece lesson on reference geometry, ocular artifact contamination, and experimental design.
4. **Reproducible Pipeline:** Auditing `generate_figures.py`, `stats_macros.tex`, and `stats_summary.json` confirms that the statistical values reported in the text and Table 1 are programmatically linked to the underlying spreadsheets (`alpha_single.xlsx`, `alpha_group.xlsx`, `eeg_spectra.xlsx`, `heart_raw.xlsx`) without manual copy-paste errors.

At the same time, from the standpoint of Muse biophysics, dry-sensor contact mechanics, spectral parameterization, and signal-processing rigor, there are several substantive methodological points that should be addressed or expanded to make this paper an authoritative reference for *Psychophysiology* readers using wearable EEG.

## Major Comments on Muse Biophysics, Referencing, Artifacts, & Spectral Analysis

### 1. Biophysical Deep-Dive into the Muse Referencing Topology (`FPz` Reference and `TP10`/Right-Ear DRL/CMS)
In Section 2.2 (lines 189–190) and Section 3.2 (lines 271–273), the authors accurately note that `TP9`, `AF7`, `AF8`, and `TP10` are differentially amplified against the mid-forehead reference (`FPz`) with driven right-leg (DRL) / common-mode sense (CMS) feedback. However, the biophysics of this specific montage warrant a more precise and complete treatment:
- **Short Forehead Inter-Electrode Distance (`AF7–FPz` and `AF8–FPz`):** Because `AF7` and `AF8` sit on the flexible forehead band only ~3–4 cm lateral to `FPz`, these channels form ultra-short bipolar-like differential derivations across the anterior frontal pole. As a result, volume-conducted cortical potentials that are spatially broad across the prefrontal cortex (as well as symmetric vertical corneo-retinal blink dipoles, as shown nicely in Figure 3A) are largely common-mode between `AF7/AF8` and `FPz` and undergo substantial phase/amplitude cancellation ($V_{\text{AF7/8}} - V_{\text{FPz}}$). What *does* survive on `AF7` and `AF8` with high gain is **local spatial gradients**: specifically, asymmetric horizontal saccades (lateral rectus EOG dipoles where `AF7` and `AF8` deflect in opposite polarity relative to `FPz`) and highly localized frontalis/corrugator/orbicularis oculi electromyographic (EMG) activity directly beneath the dry silver/ink forehead contacts.
- **Long Fronto-Temporo-Mastoid Dipoles (`TP9–FPz` and `TP10–FPz`):** Conversely, `TP9` and `TP10` sit on the conductive silicone rubber tabs behind the pinna over the mastoid/posterior-temporal bone, ~14–16 cm away from `FPz`. Thus, `TP9–FPz` and `TP10–FPz` do *not* measure isolated temporal cortex activity; they measure a **long anterior-to-posterior differential dipole spanning the entire fronto-temporo-parietal axis**. When a participant blinks, `FPz` experiences a massive positive corneo-retinal potential ($+150\text{ to }+300\ \mu\text{V}$), which enters the inverting input of the `TP9` and `TP10` differential amplifiers ($V_{\text{TP9/10}} - V_{\text{FPz}}$) and appears as a large downward/biphasic deflection on `TP9` and `TP10` (Figure 3A). Conversely, during eye closure, posterior occipito-parietal alpha generators project onto `TP9/TP10` while `FPz` acts as a relatively quiet anterior reference, allowing `TP9/TP10` to capture robust Berger alpha reactivity.
- **DRL/CMS Asymmetry on the Ear Sensor:** On the InteraXon Muse hardware, the DRL/CMS circuit is integrated into the conductive rubber ear wings (specifically coupled via the right ear / `TP10` assembly alongside the reference loop). When one ear tab loses firm skin contact or sits over hair, not only does that channel's impedance spike, but common-mode rejection across the entire 4-channel amplifier degrades, injecting 50/60 Hz mains hum and broadband noise across all channels. The manuscript should explicitly explain this DRL/CMS dependency in Section 2.1/2.2 so readers understand why seating *both* conductive silicone ear tabs (Module 1) is critical even if a user only intends to analyze frontal channels (e.g., Modules 7 and 8).

### 2. Dissecting the Module 6 vs. Module 9 Paradox: Broadband Blink Harmonics, Rectangular Window Leakage, and Band-Pass Roll-Off
The contrast between Module 6 (unguided 10-s Eyes Open vs. Eyes Closed averaged across all 4 channels: Open $5.73 \pm 5.66\ \mu\text{V}$ vs. Closed $4.67 \pm 4.81\ \mu\text{V}$, $t(82) = -2.82, p = 0.0060$) and Module 9 (60-s guided fixation with 2-s settling delay and regional separation: Temporal Closed $4.84 \pm 0.79\%$ vs. Open $4.31 \pm 0.62\%$, $t(66) = 5.62, p = 4.24 \times 10^{-7}$) is one of the most instructive findings in the paper (lines 275, 318–324). However, the spectral mechanics of *why* ocular blinks inflate the 8–12 Hz band in Module 6 should be stated even more precisely:
1. **Spectral Energy of Blinks and Saccades + Rectangular Window Sidelobe Leakage:** Although the primary energy of an ocular blink peaks at 1–5 Hz (as seen in the 5 Hz trough in Figure 4C, $t(66) = -5.74, p = 2.60 \times 10^{-7}$), `@neurosity/pipes` computes its 256-bin FFT on an **untapered rectangular window** (`epoch` $\rightarrow$ `fft` without a Hann or Hamming taper). A rectangular window has a first sidelobe at only $-13\text{ dB}$ and a slow $-6\text{ dB/octave}$ spectral leakage roll-off. When a $200\text{--}400\ \mu\text{V}$ blink or rapid eyelid flutter occurs inside a 1.0-s rectangular FFT aperture (especially on `TP9/TP10` via the `FPz` reference), spectral leakage and steep blink onset/offset edges spill several microvolts of broadband energy directly into the 8–12 Hz bins.
2. **Averaging Across All Four Sensors in Raw Microvolts:** Because `TP9` and `TP10` have $3\times\text{ to }4\times$ larger raw amplitudes than `AF7` and `AF8` (Figure 3B,C: Delta $\sim 11\text{--}12\ \mu\text{V}$ on `TP9/TP10` vs. $\sim 2\text{--}3\ \mu\text{V}$ on `AF7/AF8`), an unweighted average across all four channels in raw microvolts (Module 6) is heavily dominated by `TP9/TP10` blink leakage during unguided Eyes Open screen scanning.
3. **Opposite Aperiodic Slope Shifts Between Temporal and Frontal Sensors in Module 9:** Interestingly, if one fits a log–log $1/f$ aperiodic slope across the non-oscillatory frequencies in the `alpha_group.xlsx` dataset (`[3–6 Hz] U [17–28 Hz]`), the aperiodic amplitude exponent $\chi_A$ changes in **opposite directions** across regions during eye closure: on `TP9/TP10`, the spectrum flattens slightly from Eyes Open ($\chi_A = 1.07$) to Eyes Closed ($\chi_A = 0.99$, $t(66) = -2.83, p = 0.0061$) because high-amplitude 2–6 Hz blink potentials vanish when the eyes close; whereas on `AF7/AF8`, the spectrum *steepens* from Eyes Open ($\chi_A = 0.75$) to Eyes Closed ($\chi_A = 0.93$, $t(66) = +4.10, p = 0.00012$) because high-frequency 19–27 Hz frontalis/eyelid EMG relaxes when the eyes close! While the authors already describe the 5 Hz temporal trough and 22 Hz frontal trough in Section 3.3 (line 323), explicitly noting that these two artifact reductions rotate the broadband $1/f$ tilts in opposite directions provides an airtight justification for why their local flanking-detrended prominence metric ($\bar{A}_{8\text{--}12} - \bar{A}_{\{5\text{--}7\}\cup\{14\text{--}16\}}$) is superior to whole-spectrum relative normalization.

### 3. Individual Alpha Frequency (IAF), Fixed 8–12 Hz Band Boundaries, and High-Pass Filter Roll-Off Above 15 Hz
In my own work validating the Muse headset (Cannard et al., 2021), Individual Alpha Frequency (IAF; typically computed via the center of gravity or peak prominence between 7 and 13 Hz) is a vital trait/state marker that varies substantially across young adults (typically 8.5 to 11.5 Hz, and occasionally 7.5 to 12.5 Hz). Examining the empirical `alpha_group.xlsx` data ($N = 67$) and Figure 4A–C reveals three important phenomena regarding IAF and the fixed 8–12 Hz band that the authors should discuss and consider visualizing:
1. **Upward Overflow of Alpha Reactivity into 13–15 Hz:** Look closely at Figure 4C (the bin-by-bin paired $t$-statistic curve for `TP9/TP10`). The Bonferroni-significant positive reactivity peak ($t > 3.28$) does not stop at 12 Hz; it spans **9 to 15 Hz** (with 13 Hz $t(66) = 4.21$, 14 Hz $t(66) = 3.20$, and 15 Hz $t(66) = 3.59$). This means that in a substantial subset of undergraduate students with higher IAFs (10.5–12 Hz) or high-alpha/low-beta harmonic bursts, alpha reactivity spills directly into the upper flanking reference band (`14–16 Hz`) used for detrending! In fact, because 14–15 Hz is still significantly elevated during Eyes Closed on `TP9/TP10`, subtracting the `14–16 Hz` flank slightly *underestimates* the true alpha bump (making the reported $t(66) = 6.20$ linear bump and $t(66) = 5.96$ log-dB bump conservative lower bounds).
2. **IAF Distribution and $1/f$ Masking of Raw Argmax Peaks:** In raw or relative spectra (`CT_rel`), simply taking `argmax` across 8–12 Hz yields a downward-biased peak distribution (24/67 participants have their raw maximum at 8 Hz, 15 at 9 Hz, 18 at 10 Hz, 9 at 11 Hz, and 1 at 12 Hz; mean $9.22 \pm 1.13\text{ Hz}$) because the steep $1/f$ slope elevates the 8 Hz bin whenever a participant has a small alpha bump. Conversely, when evaluated on the **difference spectrum (`Closed - Open`)** within 8–12 Hz, the individual peak frequency centers cleanly around 10 Hz ($9.87 \pm 1.28\text{ Hz}$: 11 at 8 Hz, 17 at 9 Hz, 18 at 10 Hz, 12 at 11 Hz, 9 at 12 Hz; center-of-gravity $\text{CoG} = 9.87 \pm 0.11\text{ Hz}$ in Eyes Closed vs. $9.84 \pm 0.09\text{ Hz}$ in Eyes Open). Adding 2–3 sentences (or a supplementary/additional panel) reporting the IAF center-of-gravity and difference-peak distribution across the $N = 67$ students would directly connect EEGEdu's Module 9 output to the broader IAF literature (e.g., Corcoran et al., 2018; Cannard et al., 2021).
3. **2–20 Hz Bandpass Attenuation of the 20–30 Hz Spectrum in Module 9:** In Module 9 (`EEGEduAlpha`), the default bandpass filter is set to **2–20 Hz** (Table in Figure 1C; line 237), yet the module slices and exports integer bins from **1 to 30 Hz** (`alpha_group.xlsx`). Because `fili` applies a causal second-order Butterworth low-pass cascade at 20 Hz, frequencies from 20 to 30 Hz in Figure 4A,B are actively attenuated by the low-pass filter roll-off (which is why both curves smoothly approach $\sim 0.7\%$ per bin at 30 Hz). The authors should explicitly note in Section 3.3 and the Figure 4 caption that the 20–30 Hz tail in Module 9 reflects the roll-off of the 20 Hz low-pass filter, whereas Module 5 (`1–100 Hz`) and Module 6 (`2–50 Hz`) preserve unattenuated beta/gamma power.

### 4. Analytical Forward Models vs. Empirical Recordings in Modules 10 and 11 (Figure 5A,B)
The manuscript is commendably honest in the text (lines 337–342) and Figure 5 caption (lines 332–333) that Figure 5A (Module 10 SSVEP at 4 Hz vs. 11 Hz) and Figure 5B (Module 11 Oddball P300 ERP convergence) are **analytical forward-model simulations** parameterized to Muse noise floors (`sim_ssvep` and `p300_template` in `generate_figures.py`, lines 525–588), whereas Figure 5C (Module 12 KNN BCI) is an **empirical cross-subject validation** on the $N = 134$ classroom recordings. However:
- A reader skimming the figures or Table/Results might initially assume Figure 5A and 5B are grand-average student recordings from the classroom cohort. In Figure 5A and 5B panel titles (`A. Mod 10: SSVEP Forward Model (4 vs. 11 Hz)` and `B. Mod 11: Oddball P300 \sqrt{N} Forward Model`), the words "Forward Model" are present, which is good, but in the legends and annotations of Figure 5A,B, explicitly adding `(Synthetic Forward Model, N = 25 epochs)` or `(Monte Carlo Simulation)`—and explaining in Section 3.4 *why* classroom empirical aggregates were not available for Modules 10 and 11 (e.g., because single-session 3-hour labs completed Modules 1–6 and 9, as noted on line 182, or because standard forehead/mastoid Muse placement lacks an `Oz` sensor unless modified)—will prevent any reviewer or reader confusion.
- Furthermore, in `make_fig5_ssvep_erp_bci()` (line 541 of `generate_figures.py`), the simulated SSVEP spectrum is computed with a Hann window and squared to power (`mag = (np.abs(np.fft.rfft(sig * np.hanning(len(t_ep)))) / len(t_ep)) ** 2 * 2.0`), plotted in $\mu\text{V}^2/\text{Hz}$ with $\Delta f = 0.25\text{ Hz}$ (4-s FFT aperture), whereas the actual EEGEdu Module 10 browser code (`EEGEduSsvep`) uses an untapered 1.0-s (256-bin) FFT aperture outputting linear amplitude ($\mu\text{V}$). Please clarify this distinction in the text/caption so readers know Figure 5A illustrates the theoretical signal-to-noise ratio of a 4-s Hann-windowed SSVEP spectrum rather than the raw 1-Hz-bin `dsp.js` output.

### 5. Module 12 KNN Classifier: `StandardScaler` vs. Unit-$L_2$ (`Normalizer`) in `generate_figures.py` and Text
In Section 2.3 (line 243) and Section 3.4 (line 343), the authors accurately explain that `ml5.KNNClassifier` in the live browser application normalizes each feature vector to unit $L_2$ length ($\tilde{\mathbf{x}} = \mathbf{x} / \|\mathbf{x}\|_2$). In `generate_figures.py` (lines 639–668), the authors compute *both* `StandardScaler()` pipelines (`acc_full = 67.2%, auc_full = 0.72`; `acc_alpha = 72.4%, auc_alpha = 0.79`) and `Normalizer(norm="l2")` pipelines (`acc_l2_full = 67.9%, auc_l2_full = 0.72`; `acc_l2_alpha = 76.9%, auc_l2_alpha = 0.78`), exporting both sets of macros to `stats_macros.tex` (lines 181–190). However:
- Figure 5C plots `fpr_full, tpr_full` and `fpr_alpha, tpr_alpha` from the `StandardScaler` pipeline (`67.2% / 0.72` and `72.4% / 0.79`), and line 343 of the manuscript cites `\BCIKNNFullAcc` (`67.2%`) and `\BCIKNNAlphaAcc` (`72.4%`), while the unit-$L_2$ macros (`\BCIKNNLTwoFullAcc = 67.9%` and `\BCIKNNLTwoAlphaAcc = 76.9%`) sit unused in `stats_macros.tex`!
- Reporting the unit-$L_2$ normalized accuracy (`67.9%` full spectrum, `76.9%` alpha-prominence features, $\text{AUC} = 0.78$) alongside the $z$-scored accuracy in line 343 would both match the exact mathematical operation of `ml5.KNNClassifier` and show an even stronger out-of-sample decoding accuracy (`76.9%` vs. `72.4%`)!

## Minor Comments on Code, Figures, & Manuscript Text

1. **Module 2 & 3 Figure 2B Envelope Demodulation Detail (`generate_figures.py` lines 168–171):**
   In `make_fig2_ecg_bridge()`, `heart_raw.xlsx` was recorded in Module 2 with the 2–20 Hz bandpass filter active. Because the 2 Hz high-pass filter had already attenuated the 0.793 Hz fundamental in the exported `heart_raw.xlsx` waveform, the script reconstructs the Module 3 low-frequency fundamental (`psd_env`) via full-wave rectification (`qrs_env = np.abs(np.minimum(0, ecg_uv)) + np.maximum(0, ecg_uv)`). The caption of Figure 2B (line 250) transparently states *"against the QRS envelope-demodulated spectrum illustrating Module 3's 0.01–20 Hz passband (solid teal curve)"*. That is honest and clear, though in the main text (line 257) it would be helpful to mirror that exact phrasing ("as illustrated by envelope demodulation of the QRS pulse train in Figure 2B") so readers inspecting `generate_figures.py` appreciate why `qrs_env` was used on the `heart_raw.xlsx` trace.

2. **Sequential Epoch Timing in Figure 3D (`eeg_spectra.xlsx`):**
   In `make_fig3_multichannel_spectra()` (line 368), `t_epochs` spans roughly 0 to 10 seconds across the $N = 27$ sequential epochs (`27 * 0.3906 s = 10.16 s`), and the data come from `eeg_spectra.xlsx` (1–38 Hz at 1 Hz bins). Note that in actual Module 8 (`EEGEduSpectro`), the default parameters use a 128-sample window and 128-bin FFT ($\Delta f = 2.0\text{ Hz}$) on channel `AF7`, whereas Figure 3D plots a waterfall heatmap of `TP9` across the 27 epochs from `eeg_spectra.xlsx` (1 Hz bins, 1–38 Hz). The caption (line 266) and code comment (line 366) state *"Time–frequency waterfall spectrogram (Module 8, `EEGEduSpectro`) across the 27 sequential epochs on `TP9`"*. Please briefly note in the caption that Figure 3D renders the 27 sequential `TP9` epochs from `eeg_spectra.xlsx` as a time–frequency waterfall to allow direct visual alignment with the time-averaged spectra and band bars in Panels 3B and 3C.

3. **Typo in Live Browser Screenshot (`Figure 1A`, right screenshot):**
   In the unedited screenshot of Module 4 (`live_ui_raw.png`), the Chart.js title inside the web app reads `"Raw data from EEG electrdoes"` (spelling of *electrodes*). Obviously, since Figure 1A presents an unedited screenshot of the live web app, the figure itself is authentic, but the authors might wish to fix that one-letter typo in the `EEGEdu` GitHub repository!

4. **Missing `p`-value Macro for `FrontTwentyTwoHzT` in `stats_macros.tex`:**
   On line 123 of `stats_macros.tex` (and line 314 of `paper1_eegedu.tex`), `\FrontTwentyTwoHzT` (`-4.65`) is reported without its companion $p$-value ($p = 1.63 \times 10^{-5}$). Adding `\FrontTwentyTwoHzP` alongside `\TempFiveHzP` would make the reporting symmetric.

5. **Pre-Screened Spreadsheet Alignment in Module 2 vs. Module 3 (`line 261`):**
   On line 261, the authors note that individual-level correlation between Module 2 $\Delta\text{HR}$ and Module 3 $\Delta\text{HR}$ is $r = -0.06, p = 0.63$ *"due to non-simultaneous sequential trials and pre-screened spreadsheet row shifts"*. Could the authors clarify in one phrase what "pre-screened spreadsheet row shifts" means? Does it mean that in `alpha_single.xlsx`, the columns for Module 2 and Module 3 were compiled from separate class sheets (`heart_spectra_1.xlsx`) where missing student submissions shifted row alignment, or were they matched by student roster ID (`Column A`)? Looking at `generate_figures.py` line 832 (`both_qc = [(float(r[1]), float(r[2]), float(r[3]), float(r[4])) for r in rows_as ...]`), they are paired by row in `alpha_single.xlsx`. Clarifying whether `alpha_single.xlsx` rows are strictly aligned by student ID or independently sorted columns is important because if Columns B–C and Columns D–E are not aligned to the same student ID, a paired $t$-test (`ttest_rel(b_m2_diff, b_m3_diff)`) would be pairing unrelated rows (whereas an independent or Welch test on the two $\Delta\text{HR}$ columns would be more appropriate if row alignment was broken during pre-screening).

## Recommendation

**Minor-to-Moderate Revision.**  
This is a strong, well-engineered, and thoroughly documented methodological contribution that aligns squarely with *Psychophysiology*'s mission and its 2022 time–frequency reporting guidelines (Keil et al., 2022). Addressing the biophysical details of the `FPz` reference and ear DRL/CMS loop, expanding the discussion of IAF and the 13–15 Hz alpha overflow in Module 9, reporting the unit-$L_2$ KNN results alongside the $z$-scored KNN results, and clarifying the forward-model vs. empirical labels in Figure 5 will make this paper an outstanding resource for both researchers and educators.
