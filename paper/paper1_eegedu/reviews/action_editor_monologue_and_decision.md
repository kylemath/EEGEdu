> **[READING EXCERPT — REVIEWER #2, DR. MATTHEW B. PONTIFEX: PSYCHOMETRIC RELIABILITY vs. SPECTRAL CONSISTENCY]**
> *"Major Comment 1: Psychometric Reliability in Module 9: Spectral Consistency Across Adjacent 1 Hz FFT Bins vs. Temporal Epoch-by-Epoch Split-Half Reliability.*
> *Location: `paper1_eegedu.tex` (Section 3.3, lines 321–322) and `generate_figures.py` (lines 992–1005).*
> *The Issue: In Section 3.3 (line 321), the authors report: 'Psychometric internal consistency across the five integer alpha bins was high within both conditions, and split-half reliability of the individual alpha reactivity score across odd versus even frequency bins was excellent (Cronbach's $\alpha = 0.67$ Eyes Closed, $\alpha = 0.77$ Eyes Open; Spearman–Brown split-half $r_{\text{SB}} = 0.91$ between $\{8, 10, 12\}\text{ Hz}$ and $\{9, 11\}\text{ Hz}$).'*
> *In the psychophysiological reliability literature (e.g., Clayson & Miller, 2017; Towers & Allen, 2009; Pontifex et al., 2010), internal consistency (Cronbach's $\alpha$) and odd/even split-half reliability ($r_{\text{SB}}$) are defined across temporal trials or epochs (e.g., averaging odd-numbered 1-second FFT windows versus even-numbered 1-second FFT windows across the 60-second recording) to determine how many seconds/epochs of EEG are needed to achieve a stable trait/state estimate.*
> *Because the classroom spreadsheet (`alpha_group.xlsx`) only saved each student's 60-second condition-averaged 1–30 Hz spectrum (rather than the ~148 individual overlapping epochs per 60-second condition), `generate_figures.py` (lines 993–1004) treats the five integer frequency bins (`8, 9, 10, 11, 12 Hz`) as 'items' for Cronbach's $\alpha$ and correlates the reactivity of `{8, 10, 12} Hz` against `{9, 11} Hz` for $r_{\text{SB}} = 0.91$.*
> *Critically, correlating `{8, 10, 12} Hz` with `{9, 11} Hz` does not measure temporal measurement reliability across recording epochs. Instead, it reflects: 1. Spectral bandwidth and smoothness across the 8–12 Hz band; and 2. Deterministic spectral leakage across adjacent DFT bins caused by the rectangular window in `@neurosity/pipes` (`epoch` + `fft` without a Hann/Hamming taper, Equation 1), whose $\text{sinc}$ Dirichlet kernel has a main-lobe width of $2\Delta f = 2.0\text{ Hz}$ and substantial first-sidelobe coupling ($-13\text{ dB}$) between adjacent integer bins ($k$ and $k \pm 1$). Conversely, Cronbach's $\alpha$ across `8–12 Hz` is actually penalized during Eyes Closed ($\alpha = 0.67$ vs. $0.77$ in Eyes Open) precisely because individual differences in Individual Alpha Peak Frequency (IAF; e.g., a student peaking sharply at 9 Hz vs. 11 Hz) cause the 8 Hz and 12 Hz bins to diverge across participants when a narrowband peak emerges above the $1/f$ background!"*
