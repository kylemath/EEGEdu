# Peer Review: *Psychophysiology* (Original Article — Methodological Developments)

**Manuscript Title:** EEGEdu: A Zero-Install, Reactive WebBluetooth Platform for Human Electrophysiology, Time–Frequency Signal Processing, and Brain–Computer Interfaces  
**Authors:** Keyfer P. Mathewson, Kory W. Mathewson, and Kyle E. Mathewson  
**Reviewer:** Dr. Jennifer A. Segawa (Department of Psychology & Program in Neuroscience, Stonehill College; `jsegawa@stonehill.edu`)  
**Recommendation:** **Minor Revision** (Exceptionally strong methodological contribution; minor clarifications requested regarding classroom logistics at scale, student spreadsheet error modes, and cross-table attrition reconciliation)

---

## Overall Assessment & Compliments

This manuscript presents **EEGEdu** (`https://eegedu.com`), an open-source, zero-install Progressive Web Application (PWA) that streams 256 Hz four- and five-channel electrophysiology directly from InteraXon Muse headbands into any Chromium browser via the W3C WebBluetooth API and processes bioelectrical time series in real time using functional reactive streams (`muse-js`, `RxJS`, and `@neurosity/pipes`). The authors organize this technical stack into a 12-module curriculum spanning dry-electrode skin-contact impedance (Module 1), a Lead-I finger-pinch electrocardiogram (ECG) time-to-frequency bridge (Modules 2–3), multi-channel EEG artifact taxonomy and forehead-reference dipole biophysics (Module 4), linear FFT amplitude spectra and canonical band integration (Modules 5–6), closed-loop `p5.js` neurofeedback and rolling time–frequency spectrograms (Modules 7–8), a counterbalanced classroom Berger alpha reactivity experiment (Module 9), steady-state visual evoked potentials (SSVEPs, Module 10), visual oddball event-related potentials (ERPs, Module 11), and live in-browser $k$-nearest neighbors (KNN) brain–computer interface (BCI) classification (Module 12).

Speaking as an instructor and researcher who has spent years wrestling with low-cost EEG hardware, operating-system driver updates, and student laptop heterogeneity in undergraduate neuroscience laboratories (Segawa, 2019), I want to commend the authors for building and thoroughly validating an extraordinary pedagogical and methodological resource. Anyone who has tried to run 20 to 30 wireless EEG headsets simultaneously in a 3-hour undergraduate laboratory section knows that software installation—not hardware cost—is the primary point of failure (`BlueMuse` failing after a Windows 11 update, `muselsl` breaking across Python versions on macOS, institucional firewalls blocking Lab Streaming Layer [LSL] multicast sockets, or students arriving with iPads and Chromebooks). By executing GATT Bluetooth ingestion, causal Butterworth IIR filtering, sliding-window FFT estimation, visual stimulus presentation, KNN decoding, and client-side CSV logging entirely within a standard browser tab—paired with a one-click `mockMuseEEG(256)` synthetic stream—EEGEdu eliminates these barriers at a stroke.

Three aspects of the manuscript deserve special praise:

1. **The Lead-I Finger-Pinch ECG Time-to-Frequency Bridge (Modules 2 & 3; Section 3.1; Figure 2):** Having students remove the headband and pinch `AF7` and `FPz` between their left and right fingers to record a 10-second transthoracic Lead-I ECG before looking at cortical EEG is a masterstroke of psychophysiological pedagogy. Students frequently view the Fast Fourier Transform as an opaque mathematical black box when it is first applied to noisy, multi-component $1/f$ scalp EEG. By first counting their own R-wave peaks in the time domain (Module 2) and immediately seeing that exact rate—alongside its $2f_0$ and $3f_0$ QRS impulse harmonics and the effect of a 2.0 Hz (120 BPM) high-pass cutoff—appear in an 8,192-bin zero-padded BPM spectrum (Module 3), students gain an unmistakable physical anchor for frequency decomposition.
2. **Transparent Instruction on Reference Geometry and Methodological Confounds (Sections 3.2 & 3.3; Figures 3 & 4):** Rather than hiding the quirks of consumer dry-electrode EEG, the authors turn them into quantitative lessons. Their explanation of why eye blinks appear fourfold larger on posterior-temporal (`TP9/TP10`) than anterior-frontal (`AF7/AF8`) Muse channels—because `AF7/AF8` sit 3–4 cm from the `FPz` reference where corneo-retinal potentials are largely common-mode, whereas `TP9/TP10` sit ~14 cm away behind the ears and measure the inverted forehead dipole—is crystal clear. Equally valuable is the candid reporting of the Module 6 unguided 10-second exercise ($N = 83$), where apparent 8–12 Hz amplitude was *higher* during Eyes Open than Eyes Closed ($t(82) = -2.82, p = 0.0060$) because students blinked and scanned their laptop screens during the brief unguided window. Contrastive reporting of this failure against the successful 60-second fixation-controlled Module 9 protocol ($N = 67, t(66) = 5.62, p = 4.24 \times 10^{-7}$; 80.6% single-student replication in flanking-detrended prominence) is a model of anti-Goodharting scientific integrity.
3. **Mathematical Rigor and Adherence to *Psychophysiology* Guidelines:** The manuscript explicitly reconciles `@neurosity/pipes`'s one-sided linear spectral amplitude ($\hat{A}_c[k] = \frac{2}{N_{\text{FFT}}}|X_c[k]|$ in $\mu\text{V}$, Equation 1) with conventional power spectral density ($\hat{S}_c[k]$ in $\mu\text{V}^2/\text{Hz}$, Equation 2), explains the 1,024-sample buffer vs. trailing 256-sample FFT slice ($156/256 = 60.9\%$ overlap), and validates narrowband alpha prominence in both linear relative coordinates and scale-invariant log-decibel ($10\log_{10}$) coordinates following Keil et al. (2022) and Donoghue et al. (2020).

The manuscript is exceptionally well written, the five figures and Table 1 are publication-ready, and the statistical pipeline (`generate_figures.py` $\rightarrow$ `stats_macros.tex`) is fully reproducible. My comments below focus on practical classroom deployment details, novice student error modes, and sample-size/attrition transparency that will help fellow educators and field researchers replicate this work seamlessly at their own institutions.

## Major Comments on Classroom Deployment, Novice Error Modes, & Methodological Replicability

### 1. Classroom Logistics at Scale: BLE 2.4 GHz Congestion, Headband Rotation Hygiene, Battery Management, and Browser Compatibility (Section 2.1, pp. 4–5)
In Section 2.1 (lines 181–183), the authors note that in sections of 30 to 80 students sharing 15 to 25 wireless headbands, headsets were labeled with their factory four-character hexadecimal suffix (`Muse-XXXX`) and paired in staggered 30-second rows. Having deployed wireless EEG headsets in undergraduate classrooms myself, I know that instructors adopting EEGEdu at other institutions will immediately ask several practical operational questions that warrant a brief expansion (either in Section 2.1 or as a short operational checklist in the Discussion):
- **2.4 GHz RF Congestion & Packet Drops:** When 15 to 25 Muse headbands stream 256 Hz notifications simultaneously (five GATT characteristics emitting 12-sample packets every 46.875 ms $\approx 107\text{ packets/s}$ per headset) alongside 30–80 student laptops and smartphones connected to campus Wi-Fi in an unshielded lecture hall, how frequently do WebBluetooth GATT disconnections or `zipSamples` packet-alignment lags occur, and how does EEGEdu recover when a headset momentarily drops packets?
- **Headband Rotation, Hygiene, and Battery Depletion:** When 15–25 headsets are rotated across $N = 86$ student operators within or across 3-hour lab periods, how is headband sanitization handled between partners (e.g., alcohol-free vs. isopropyl wipes on the conductive silicone mastoid tabs and forehead band, given that alcohol can dry out or degrade conductive rubber over repeated semesters)? Furthermore, how long does a charge last under continuous WebBluetooth GATT streaming, and do instructors need to un-pair (`Disconnect` button) before the next student in a pair can discover the `Muse-XXXX` device in Chrome's chooser dialog? (A very common novice pitfall with BLE devices is that once Student A pairs a Muse headset, it stops advertising and disappears from Student B's WebBluetooth dialog—or gets auto-claimed by the host OS Bluetooth menu rather than the browser tab!).
- **Browser/OS Edge Cases (`Bluefy` on iOS, Linux/Windows BLE permissions):** In Section 2.2 (line 187), the authors mention Chrome, Edge, Android, ChromeOS, and `Bluefy` on iOS/iPadOS. Does `FileSaver.js` client-side CSV export (`Blob` download) work reliably inside `Bluefy` on iOS/iPadOS so students on iPads can export spreadsheets to Google Sheets, or is iPad usage primarily recommended for live visualization (Modules 1, 4, 7, 8) while laptops handle CSV logging? Clarifying this in 1–2 sentences will save adopting instructors significant troubleshooting time.

### 2. Novice Operator & Spreadsheet Error Modes: Reconciling Pre-Screening vs. Threshold Exclusions in Modules 2 and 3 (Section 3.1, pp. 9–11; Table 1)
The comparison between manual time-domain peak counting in Module 2 (`EEGEduHeartRaw`) and automated frequency-domain peak tracking in Module 3 (`EEGEduHeartSpectra`) is one of the most instructive parts of the paper, but the exact two-stage error-filtering workflow between the raw class spreadsheet (`heart_spectra_1.xlsx`) and the analysis sheet (`alpha_single.xlsx`) should be described slightly more explicitly in Section 3.1 so readers understand *how* novice students make mistakes in Module 2 versus why Module 3 achieves 100% retention ($N = 76/76$).
- Specifically, line 259 states: *"($N = 86$ roster IDs in `alpha_single.xlsx`, pre-screened from `heart_spectra_1.xlsx`). In Module 2, where students manually counted R-wave peaks using spreadsheet voltage thresholds, heart rate increased significantly from sitting to standing after standard physiological quality control ($N = 67$ valid pairs out of 75 submissions, 89.3% retention after excluding 8 missed-beat rows below 40 BPM...)"* and then presents the symmetric $[30, 200]\text{ BPM}$ sensitivity check ($N = 75$, 100% of numerical submissions in `alpha_single.xlsx`).
- Inspecting the underlying repository spreadsheets reveals *two* distinct novice error mechanisms in Module 2 that are pedagogically fascinating:
  1. **Gross formula/threshold entry errors in `heart_spectra_1.xlsx` (the 11 rows blanked out as missing in `alpha_single.xlsx`):** In the raw class sheet (`heart_spectra_1.xlsx`), several students entered physically impossible numbers such as $765.3\text{ BPM}$, $584.7\text{ BPM}$, $7272.7\text{ BPM}$, $0.0031\text{ BPM}$, or negative values ($-35.2\text{ BPM}, -308.3\text{ BPM}, -1500\text{ BPM}$) because they either left the spreadsheet peak-detection threshold inside the baseline noise band (counting every 256 Hz noise oscillation), divided elapsed milliseconds the wrong way when converting inter-beat intervals to BPM, or subtracted timestamps in reverse order. Those gross formula errors were pre-screened out (set to empty/missing in `alpha_single.xlsx`, yielding the 75 numerical pairs and 11 missing rows out of $N = 86$).
  2. **Missed R-wave peak errors within `alpha_single.xlsx` (the 8 rows between 30 and 40 BPM):** Among the 75 numerical submissions in `alpha_single.xlsx`, 8 rows had a sitting or standing value between $31.74$ and $38.87\text{ BPM}$ (specifically IDs 110, 123, 130, 135, 149, 163, 164, and 184)—which happens when a student sets their manual voltage threshold slightly too high and misses 2 or 3 R-wave peaks in the 10-second trace (e.g., counting 5 or 6 beats in 10 s $\rightarrow 30\text{–}36\text{ BPM}$), or clicks export before the 10-second buffer is fully populated with clean beats.
- **Concrete Suggestion:** Briefly state both novice error mechanisms in Section 3.1 (lines 259–261)! Explaining that (a) 11 raw spreadsheet entries in `heart_spectra_1.xlsx` were blanked prior to `alpha_single.xlsx` due to gross student formula/noise-threshold errors (e.g., $>400\text{ BPM}$, $\approx 0\text{ BPM}$, or negative BPMs from inverted interval formulas), and (b) 8 of the 75 numerical pairs in `alpha_single.xlsx` fell between $31.7$ and $38.9\text{ BPM}$ due to high voltage thresholds missing R-wave peaks (which is why the $[30, 200]\text{ BPM}$ symmetric check retains all $N = 75$ pairs!), makes the contrast with Module 3's 100% automated FFT retention ($N = 76/76$) even more compelling and explains to readers exactly how `heart_spectra_1.xlsx` maps to `alpha_single.xlsx`.
- Relatedly, on line 261, the authors note that the individual-level correlation between Module 2 $\Delta\text{HR}$ and Module 3 $\Delta\text{HR}$ across the $N = 59$ students with valid data in all four columns is $r = -0.06, p = 0.63$ *"due to non-simultaneous sequential trials and pre-screened spreadsheet row shifts."* Inspecting row 49 (`ID 149` / `150`) of `heart_spectra_1.xlsx` vs. `alpha_single.xlsx` confirms that deleting two rows in the raw M2 columns shifted Lab IDs 150–188 by two rows relative to the M3/M6 columns in `alpha_single.xlsx`! This is a remarkably honest catch by the authors' auditing pipeline—expanding this phrase by half a sentence so the reader understands that paired within-module posture columns (`Stand` vs. `Sit` in Mod 2; `Stand` vs. `Sit` in Mod 3; `Open` vs. `Closed` in Mod 6) remained strictly aligned within each student, while a two-row offset at ID 150 decoupled cross-module row alignment between Mod 2 and Mod 3, will make that parenthetical completely transparent.

### 3. Comprehensive Sample-Size & Attrition Accounting Across Modules 2, 3, 6, and 9 (Sections 2.1, 3.1–3.3, Discussion, and Table 1)
All statistical values, degrees of freedom, $t$-statistics, $p$-values, effect sizes ($d_z$), and 95% confidence intervals are 100% consistent across the Abstract, Methods, Results, Discussion, Figures 2–5, and Table 1. However, a reader comparing sample sizes across modules ($N = 86$ roster IDs; $N = 75$ submitted / $67$ retained in Mod 2; $N = 76$ in Mod 3; $N = 66$ raw / $59$ QC-retained in Mod 2 $\cap$ Mod 3; $N = 83$ in Mod 6; and $N = 67$ complete 4-condition datasets out of 77–78 per-sheet submissions in Mod 9) may wonder why attrition varies across modules within the same cohort.
- In particular, in Module 9 (`alpha_group.xlsx`, Section 3.3, line 319), the text states: *"$N = 67$ complete four-condition participants out of 77–78 per-sheet submissions and 86 roster IDs in `alpha_group.xlsx`."*
- Inspecting `alpha_group.xlsx` shows why 77–78 valid 30-bin spectra per sheet intersect to $N = 67$ complete four-condition participants:
  - `OpenFrontal` has 77 complete 30-bin rows (plus 1 partial 29-bin row for ID 113 where the 30 Hz cell was omitted during copy-paste);
  - `OpenTemporal` has 77 complete 30-bin rows (plus 1 partial 29-bin row for ID 113);
  - `ClosedFrontal` has 78 complete 30-bin rows;
  - `ClosedTemporal` has 78 complete 30-bin rows.
  - Across all four sheets, 87 unique student IDs submitted at least one complete 30-bin condition, and 77 unique IDs submitted at least two conditions, but 9 students (`IDs 178–186`) pasted their `OpenTemporal`, `ClosedFrontal`, and `ClosedTemporal` rows while leaving `OpenFrontal` blank, 8 students (`IDs 121, 122, 126, 131, 148, 149, 159, 188`) only pasted `OpenFrontal`, 1 student (`ID 145`) only pasted the two `Open` sheets, 1 student (`ID 155`) missed `OpenTemporal`, and 1 student (`ID 113`) missed the 30th frequency bin (`30 Hz`) on the two `Open` sheets—leaving exactly $N = 67$ participants with complete 30-bin spectra across all four sheets (`OpenFrontal`, `OpenTemporal`, `ClosedFrontal`, `ClosedTemporal`).
- **Concrete Suggestion:** Add 1–2 sentences in Section 3.3 (around line 319) or the Table 1 footnote explaining that the reduction from 77–78 valid submissions per sheet to $N = 67$ complete four-condition participants in Module 9 was driven by Google Sheets copy-paste attrition across the four separate workbook tabs (e.g., 9 students omitting the `OpenFrontal` tab and 1 student truncating the 30 Hz column on the `Open` tabs), rather than hardware failure during the 60-second recordings. For fellow educators, this is a crucial practical takeaway: when 80 students simultaneously paste 30-column CSV averages into a shared multi-tab Google Sheet at the end of a lab period, tab-switching and column-selection errors are the primary source of missing data! You might even note that future classroom deployments can eliminate this copy-paste attrition by having EEGEdu export a single tidy summary row per student across all four conditions.

## Minor Comments on Text, Figures, Table 1, & Formatting

1. **Figure 1A Screenshot Typo Note (Page 3, `fig1_eegedu_curriculum_architecture.png`):**
   - In Figure 1A (top-right screenshot of the live deployed app at `https://eegedu.com`), the Chart.js title inside the screenshot reads `"Raw data from EEG electrdoes"` (a charming typo in the upstream web app's chart title string) and `"Enable Muse Auxillary Channel"` in the UI control panel, and the two gray buttons both say `"Mock Data Connected"` while active. Since the caption rightly describes these as *"two unedited screenshots of the deployed web application at `https://eegedu.com`,"* no change to the screenshot is required, though the authors may wish to fix `"electrdoes"` $\rightarrow$ `"electrodes"` and `"Auxillary"` $\rightarrow$ `"Auxiliary"` in a future commit to the `kylemath/EEGEdu` repository.

2. **Clarifying Figure 2B Envelope-Demodulated Spectrum vs. Live Module 3 Pipeline (Page 10, `fig2_ecg_time_to_frequency_bridge.png`, line 250 & `generate_figures.py` lines 168–171):**
   - The Figure 2B caption (line 250) admirably and accurately states: *"contrasting the Module 2 high-pass trace ($>2.0\text{ Hz} = 120\text{ BPM}$ cutoff, purple dotted curve, which attenuates the fundamental and leaves higher harmonics) against the QRS envelope-demodulated spectrum illustrating Module 3's $0.01\text{–}20\text{ Hz}$ passband (solid teal curve), which recovers the true cardiac fundamental ($f_0 = 0.793\text{ Hz} = 47.6\text{ BPM}$)."*
   - Because `heart_raw.xlsx` was recorded in Module 2 (where the $2\text{–}20\text{ Hz}$ bandpass filter was already applied in real time prior to CSV export), reconstructing the $f_0 = 47.6\text{ BPM}$ fundamental peak from `heart_raw.xlsx` in `generate_figures.py` required full-wave rectification (`qrs_env = np.abs(np.minimum(0, ecg_uv)) + np.maximum(0, ecg_uv)`) to demodulate the R-wave pulse train back to its fundamental repetition rate. While the caption already explicitly discloses *"the QRS envelope-demodulated spectrum illustrating Module 3's $0.01\text{–}20\text{ Hz}$ passband,"* adding a brief parenthetical in the main text (Section 3.1, line 257) noting that Figure 2B uses envelope demodulation on the Module 2 export (`heart_raw.xlsx`) to visualize the $f_0 = 47.6\text{ BPM}$ fundamental that Module 3's live $0.01\text{–}20\text{ Hz}$ passband preserves directly will preempt any question from a reader who inspects `generate_figures.py`.

3. **Module 12 Feature Representation in Live App vs. Offline Benchmark (Section 2.3, line 243; Section 3.4, line 343; Figure 5C):**
   - In Section 2.3 (line 243), the live browser classifier (`EEGEduPredict`) is described as training `ml5.KNNClassifier` ($k = 3$, unit-$L_2$ normalization) on 30-bin `TP9` ($1\text{–}30\text{ Hz}$) spectral vectors. In Section 3.4 (line 343) and Figure 5C, the offline subject-grouped 5-fold cross-validation (`GroupKFold`) benchmark on `alpha_group.xlsx` ($N = 134$ recordings from 67 students) evaluates `StandardScaler` + distance-weighted KNN on the 60-bin bilateral spectra ($k = 5$, $\text{Acc} = 67.2\%, \text{AUC} = 0.72$) and on a 13-feature narrowband alpha-prominence vector ($k = 7$, $\text{Acc} = 72.4\%, \text{AUC} = 0.79$).
   - I noticed in `stats_macros.tex` (lines 185–190) and `generate_figures.py` (lines 654–668) that the authors *also* computed the exact unit-$L_2$ normalized (`Normalizer(norm="l2")`, matching `ml5.KNNClassifier`) cross-validated benchmark, which actually achieves even higher accuracy on the alpha-prominence features ($\text{Acc} = 76.9\%, \text{AUC} = 0.78$ via `\BCIKNNLTwoAlphaAcc` / `\BCIKNNLTwoAlphaAUC`, and $\text{Acc} = 67.9\%, \text{AUC} = 0.72$ on the full 60-bin spectra via `\BCIKNNLTwoFullAcc`)! Mentioning those unit-$L_2$ macros alongside the $z$-scored KNN numbers in Section 3.4 (line 343) would be a great way to connect the offline `GroupKFold` benchmark directly to `ml5.KNNClassifier`'s internal unit-$L_2$ normalization.

4. **Modules 10 & 11 Analytical Forward Models vs. Classroom Constraints (Section 3.4, pp. 16–17; Figure 5A,B):**
   - The authors are scrupulously transparent in both the text (lines 337–341) and the Figure 5 caption (lines 332–333) that Panels 5A (Module 10 SSVEP) and 5B (Module 11 Oddball ERP) are *"analytical forward-model benchmarks... parameterized to empirical Muse noise levels,"* whereas Panel 5C (Module 12 BCI) is empirical classroom data ($N = 134$ recordings). From a classroom time-budget perspective, it would be helpful for adopting instructors to note in Section 2.1 or 3.4 why Modules 10 and 11 are typically demonstrated or analyzed via forward models / instructor demos rather than full-class Google Sheets aggregation: namely, within a single 3-hour introductory lab period, students focus on Modules 1–6 and 9, whereas recording clean SSVEPs (Module 10) requires repositioning the headband over occipital cortex (`Oz`/`O1`/`O2`) or attaching a 5th `AUX` electrode, and recording a 100-target oddball ERP (Module 11; 400 total trials at ~1.5 s SOA $\approx 10\text{ min}$ of continuous unblinking fixation plus offline trial epoching) is best suited for an advanced multi-week lab section.

5. **Table 1 & Typography Check Across Compiled PDF Pages (`page-01.png` to `page-20.png`):**
   - I visually inspected all 20 compiled pages of `paper1_eegedu.pdf`. There are zero overfull boxes, zero clipped axis labels, and zero overlapping annotations across all five figures (`fig1` to `fig5`) and Table 1.
   - In Table 1 (page 13, line 291–295), notice that for Modules 2 and 3, Condition 1 is `Standing` and Condition 2 is `Sitting`, whereas for Module 6 (line 296) and Module 9 (lines 298–305), Condition 1 is `Closed` and Condition 2 is `Open` (and the parenthetical labels `(Closed)` and `(Open)` are helpfully printed right next to every Mean $\pm$ SD in the table cells!). For complete parallel symmetry, you could optionally add `(Stand)` and `(Sit)` next to the Mean $\pm$ SD entries in the first two rows of Table 1 just as you did for `(Closed)` and `(Open)` in the lower rows.

---

## Recommendation

**Minor Revision.** This is a landmark methodological and pedagogical contribution to *Psychophysiology*. EEGEdu solves a pressing, universal bottleneck in hands-on human electrophysiology instruction and scalable out-of-the-lab research, and the empirical validation across cardiac and cortical modules is rigorous, transparent, and insightful. Addressing the minor points above—particularly expanding the practical guidance on classroom BLE management and explicitly describing the two-stage spreadsheet error modes and tab-level attrition—will make this paper an indispensable reference for psychophysiology educators and researchers worldwide.
