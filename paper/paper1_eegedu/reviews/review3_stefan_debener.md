## Overall Assessment & Compliments

In this methodological report, Mathewson and colleagues present **EEGEdu** (`https://eegedu.com`), an open-source, zero-install Progressive Web Application (PWA) that streams 256 Hz bioelectrical signals from consumer wireless EEG headbands (InteraXon Muse 2016, Muse 2, and Muse S) directly into standard Chromium web browsers via the W3C WebBluetooth API and processes them in real time using functional reactive streams (`RxJS` and `@neurosity/pipes`). Across a 12-module curriculum validated in large undergraduate university classrooms ($N = 86$ roster IDs), the authors demonstrate:
1. A clever cross-modal **Einthoven Lead-I ECG fingertip pinch paradigm** (Modules 2 and 3; `AF7` vs. `FPz`) that bridges time-domain R-wave counting and frequency-domain Fourier harmonic analysis across an orthostatic posture challenge (Sitting vs. Standing);
2. **Multi-channel artifact taxonomy and differential reference biophysics** (Modules 4–8), clearly explaining why a mid-forehead reference (`FPz`) makes corneo-retinal blink dipoles appear small at adjacent forehead sites (`AF7`/`AF8`) and large and inverted ($\sim 205\ \mu\text{V}$) at distant temporo-mastoid sites (`TP9`/`TP10`);
3. **Classroom replication of the Berger (1929) alpha reactivity effect** (Module 9; $N = 67$ complete 4-condition datasets), including an instructive contrast against an unguided 10-second baseline exercise confounded by ocular artifacts (Module 6; $N = 83$), paired with rigorous parameterization in both linear relative coordinates and scale-invariant log-decibel ($10\log_{10}$) flanking-detrended prominence; and
4. **In-browser brain–computer interface (BCI) paradigms** spanning steady-state visual evoked potentials (SSVEPs; Module 10), visual oddball event-related potentials (ERPs; Module 11), and live $k$-nearest neighbors (`ml5.KNNClassifier`) spectral decoding (Module 12).

Having spent the past fifteen years working to move electroencephalography "beyond the laboratory" using wireless amplifiers, smartphone acquisition, dry and transparent around-ear sensors (cEEGrid), and ambulatory BCIs, I want to congratulate the authors on a genuinely important engineering and pedagogical contribution. Anyone who has tried to run a 20-headset mobile EEG workshop or classroom lab knows that the biggest point of failure is rarely the amplifier hardware—it is operating-system driver permissions, broken Python/LSL dependencies (`muselsl`, `BlueMuse`), local firewall blocks on multicast UDP sockets, and heterogeneous student laptops (Windows, macOS, ChromeOS). Eliminating the entire native middleware stack by running GATT packet ingestion, causal Butterworth filtering, FFT periodogram estimation, stimulus presentation, and KNN classification inside a sandboxed browser tab is a major milestone for scalable neurophysiology.

Moreover, I deeply appreciate the scientific honesty of the manuscript. Rather than hiding the quirks of consumer dry-electrode hardware—such as the `FPz` reference geometry, the reverse ocular confound in the unguided 10-second Module 6 exercise ($t(82) = -2.82, p = 0.0060$), or the $-21\text{ BPM}$ baseline offset in Module 3's automated spectral tracker—the authors turn every hardware constraint into a quantitative signal-processing lesson.

To make this paper an enduring methodological reference for the mobile EEG, psychophysiology, and BCI communities in *Psychophysiology*, I have **five Major Comments** and **eight Minor Comments** focused on wireless BLE packet transport, browser-side ERP trigger jitter, dry-electrode Lead-I ECG biophysics, and online vs. offline KNN BCI decoding. Every comment below includes concrete, actionable solutions with exact file and line references.

## Major Comments on Wireless Hardware, Timing, Biophysics, & BCI

### 1. Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) Packet Framing, `Date.now()` Arrival Timestamping, and the Analytical Transfer Function of Trigger Jitter on ERPs (Module 11)
* **Files & Lines:** `paper1_eegedu.tex` (Section 2.2, lines 189–190; Section 2.3, line 241; Section 3.4, line 341; Section 4, line 348), `generate_figures.py` (lines 558–588, 703–733, Figure 5B), and `repos/EEGEdu/src/components/PageSwitcher/components/EEGEduEvoked/EEGEduEvoked.js` (lines 47–56, 299–315) & `sketchEvoked.js` (lines 31–68).
* **Technical Issue:**  
  In Wireless EEG, timing is everything—especially when transitioning from stationary spectral power (where phase is discarded) to phase-locked event-related potentials (Module 11). Let us trace the exact hardware-to-browser timing chain in EEGEdu:
  1. **BLE GATT Framing:** On the Muse hardware (`0xFE8D`), each EEG notification across characteristics `273e0003`–`273e0007` packs **12 unsigned 12-bit integer samples** from a single electrode (scaled by $0.48828125\ \mu\text{V}/\text{LSB}$). At $f_s = 256\text{ Hz}$, 12 samples represent a fixed packet duration of:
     $$T_{\text{packet}} = \frac{12}{256\text{ Hz}} = 46.875\text{ ms} \quad (f_{\text{packet}} = 21.33\text{ Hz}).$$
  2. **Multi-Channel Zipping & Burst Emission:** In `EEGEduEvoked.js` (line 47), `zipSamples(window.source.eegReadings$)` waits until a 12-sample packet has arrived from each active electrode and then synchronously unrolls and emits all 12 multi-channel samples in an immediate JavaScript microtask burst.
  3. **Browser Trigger Latching (`window.marker`) and `Date.now()` Stamping:** In `sketchEvoked.js` (lines 31–65), visual oddball stimuli are drawn inside the `p5.js` `draw()` loop governed by `requestAnimationFrame` at 60 Hz ($16.67\text{ ms}$ frame interval), which sets a global variable `window.marker = 20` (target) or `10` (standard). In `EEGEduEvoked.js` (lines 300–312), `epoch({ duration: 1, interval: 1 })` emits each sample to `saveToCSV`, which stamps `Date.now()`, reads `window.marker`, and immediately resets `window.marker = 0` on the **first sample of the next arriving 12-sample BLE burst**.
  4. **Consequences for Csv Timestamps & Phase-Locked ERPs:** Because all 12 samples within a BLE packet are emitted in a single synchronous JavaScript turn when the last channel notification arrives, all 12 rows in the exported CSV receive virtually the **same** `Date.now()` millisecond timestamp (stepwise jumps of $\sim 47\text{ ms}$ every 12 rows rather than uniform $3.90625\text{ ms}$ increments), and the stimulus onset marker (`10` or `20`) is latched to sample $0$ of the newly arrived 12-sample packet—even though the visual flash occurred uniformly at random within the $46.875\text{ ms}$ packet accumulation window, plus OS Bluetooth stack connection-interval jitter ($7.5\text{--}30\text{ ms}$) and display vsync quantization ($16.67\text{ ms}$).
  5. **Mismatch with the Analytical Forward Model in Figure 5B:** In `paper1_eegedu.tex` (line 341), the authors briefly note that *"bundled Bluetooth packet arrival introduces 15 to 45 ms of trigger latency jitter... which low-pass smooths rapid early sensory peaks while preserving broader cognitive components like the P300."* However, in `generate_figures.py` (lines 559–566), the analytical forward model plotted in **Figure 5B** generates every trial with **zero latency jitter** ($\sigma_\tau = 0\text{ ms}$):
     ```python
     target_trials = p300_template[None, :] + rng.normal(0, single_trial_noise_sd, size=(n_max_trials, len(t_erp_ms)))
     ```
     Mathematically, if single-trial latency $\tau_i \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_\tau, \sigma_\tau^2)$ (or uniform across $[0, T_{\text{packet}}]$ plus Gaussian stack jitter), the expected ensemble-averaged ERP $\mathbb{E}[\bar{x}(t)] = (s * p_\tau)(t)$ is convolved with the jitter density $p_\tau(\tau)$, which multiplies the ERP spectrum by the low-pass transfer function:
     $$|H_{\text{jitter}}(f)| = \left|\text{sinc}(f T_{\text{packet}})\right| \cdot \exp\left(-2\pi^2 f^2 \sigma_{\text{BLE}}^2\right).$$
     For $\sigma_{\text{total}} \approx 20\text{--}25\text{ ms}$ (or $T_{\text{packet}} = 46.875\text{ ms}$, whose first spectral null is at $21.33\text{ Hz}$ and $-3\text{ dB}$ point is at $\sim 9.4\text{ Hz}$), an exogenous N170 peak ($\sigma_t = 28\text{ ms}$, dominant energy $\sim 6\text{--}12\text{ Hz}$) loses $\sim 25\text{--}45\%$ of its peak amplitude and broadens temporally, whereas the slow P300 ($\sigma_t = 55\text{ ms}$, dominant energy $< 3\text{ Hz}$) retains $> 90\%$ of its amplitude!
* **Concrete Solution:**
  1. In **Section 2.2** (lines 189–190) or **Section 3.4** (line 341), explicitly state the 12-sample BLE packet duration ($12 / 256\text{ Hz} = 46.875\text{ ms}$, $21.33\text{ Hz}$ packet rate) and explain that browser-side `Date.now()` logging in Module 11 assigns burst-arrival timestamps to 12-sample blocks, meaning offline ERP epoching must index time by sample row count ($\Delta t = 1/256\text{ s} = 3.90625\text{ ms/row}$) relative to the trigger row rather than using the stepped `Timestamp (ms)` column directly.
  2. In **Section 3.4** (or Figure 5B), state the equivalent low-pass cutoff of the $15\text{--}45\text{ ms}$ BLE/display trigger jitter ($\text{sinc}(f T_{\text{packet}})$ / Gaussian smoothing kernel, $f_{-3\text{dB}} \approx 6\text{--}10\text{ Hz}$) to give readers a rigorous mathematical explanation of why WebBluetooth ERPs reliably preserve slow endogenous components (P300, LPP) while attenuating sharp exogenous peaks (C1, P1, N1/N170).

### 2. Cross-Modal Einthoven Lead-I ECG Biophysics and the $-21\text{ BPM}$ Spectral vs. Time-Domain Baseline Discrepancy (Modules 2 & 3)
* **Files & Lines:** `paper1_eegedu.tex` (Section 2.3, lines 223–226; Section 3.1, lines 254–262; Figure 2 caption, lines 249–252), `generate_figures.py` (lines 168–180, 201–227), `repos/EEGEdu/src/components/PageSwitcher/components/EEGEduHeartSpectra/EEGEduHeartSpectra.js` (lines 27–39, 139–143, 480–503, 588–590), and `node_modules/@neurosity/pipes/dist/eeg-pipes.umd.js` (`fft.js` / `zeroPad.js`).
* **Technical Issue:**  
  The fingertip-pinch Lead-I ECG bridge (Modules 2 and 3) is one of the cleverest pedagogical innovations in the paper, and the concordant within-subject orthostatic shift across the $N = 59$ intersection ($\Delta\text{HR}_{\text{Mod 2}} = +9.59 \pm 31.30\text{ BPM}$ vs. $\Delta\text{HR}_{\text{Mod 3}} = +9.75 \pm 21.85\text{ BPM}$, $t(58) = -0.03, p = 0.974, d_z = 0.00$) is remarkably convincing. However, three biophysical and signal-processing aspects of Modules 2 and 3 deserve fuller precision:
  1. **Floating Driven Right Leg (`CMS/DRL`) During Two-Finger Pinch:** On the scalp, the Muse headset uses the right temporo-mastoid electrode (`TP10`) as the active Driven Right Leg (`DRL`) common-mode feedback electrode to suppress $50/60\text{ Hz}$ mains interference. When a student removes the headset from their head and pinches only `AF7` (left hand) and `FPz` (right hand) as instructed in `EEGEduHeartRaw.js` (lines 187–190), the `DRL` electrode on the ear is floating in air! Consequently, two-electrode transthoracic recording relies entirely on the instrumentation amplifier's raw common-mode rejection across mismatched dry electrode impedances (silver-coated `AF7` vs. conductive silicone `FPz`), which is why the $20\text{ Hz}$ low-pass filter (`cutOffHigh: 20`) is vital to suppress large $60\text{ Hz}$ power-line pickup. (In our own mobile physiology labs, touching a second finger of the right hand to `TP10` re-engages the `DRL` loop and dramatically stabilizes the baseline!)
  2. **Why Module 3 Sitting HR is $56.43\text{ BPM}$ vs. Module 2 Sitting HR of $77.85\text{ BPM}$:** In `alpha_single.xlsx`, 26 out of 76 students in Module 3 Sitting have recorded heart rates between $30.15$ and $45.0\text{ BPM}$ (10th percentile $= 35.7\text{ BPM}$, 25th percentile $= 41.8\text{ BPM}$, median $= 53.2\text{ BPM}$). Examining the actual JavaScript implementation in `@neurosity/pipes` (`fft.js`) and `EEGEduHeartSpectra.js` reveals **three compounding mechanisms** behind this downward shift:
     - **Mechanism A (Zero-Padding Step Discontinuity on Un-detrended Rectangular Windows):** In `EEGEduHeartSpectra.js` (lines 28–36), `duration: 2048` (8.0 s) is piped into `fft({ bins: 8192 })`. Inside `@neurosity/pipes`, `zeroPad(safeSamples, 8192)` appends 6,144 zeros directly to the raw 2,048-sample buffer **without** subtracting the 8-second window mean and **without** applying a Hann taper. Because the high-pass cutoff is $0.01\text{ Hz}$ ($\tau = 1 / [2\pi \cdot 0.01] \approx 15.9\text{ s}$), any residual DC offset, slow finger-pressure baseline drift, or respiratory sinus arrhythmia / respiratory baseline wander ($0.15\text{--}0.4\text{ Hz}$) creates a step discontinuity at sample 2,048 whose Dirichlet ($\text{sinc}$) sidelobes peak strongly at the lowest bins of `sliceFFT([0.5, 2.5])` ($0.50\text{--}0.75\text{ Hz} = 30\text{--}45\text{ BPM}$). Furthermore, `dsp.js` divides the zero-padded spectrum by `bufferSize = 8192` rather than $N_{\text{win}} = 2048$, scaling amplitudes by $2048/8192 = 0.25$.
     - **Mechanism B (Biphasic QRS Harmonic Dominance vs. Student `/2` Harmonic Correction):** Because the Lead-I QRS complex is sharply biphasic (positive R-wave of $+230\ \mu\text{V}$ and negative S-wave of $-360\ \mu\text{V}$ in Figure 2A), its linear fundamental $f_0$ has much less energy than a rectified pulse train, whereas its 2nd and 3rd harmonics ($2f_0, 3f_0$) are large. In `EEGEduHeartSpectra.js` (lines 486, 498–503) and the linked instructional spreadsheet (`heart_spectra_2.xlsx`, rows 22 and 27, Column D `=C22/2 = 30.9375`), students were explicitly instructed to correct harmonic jumps near $120\text{ BPM}$ by dividing by 2 (`=C22/2`). When students applied `/2` to windows that were actually tracking a normal fundamental (e.g., $70\text{--}85\text{ BPM} \div 2 = 35\text{--}42.5\text{ BPM}$) or when `indexOfMax` locked onto the $30\text{--}45\text{ BPM}$ baseline drift edge, the resulting values clustered in the $30\text{--}45\text{ BPM}$ band.
  3. **Full-Wave Rectification in Figure 2B vs. Linear FFT in Module 3:** In `generate_figures.py` (lines 168–170), the solid teal curve in Figure 2B (`psd_env`) is computed by taking the Hann-windowed FFT of the **full-wave rectified QRS envelope** (`qrs_env = np.abs(np.minimum(0, ecg_uv)) + np.maximum(0, ecg_uv)`) of `heart_raw.xlsx` (because `heart_raw.xlsx` was recorded in Module 2 with a $2\text{ Hz}$ high-pass filter that stripped the linear fundamental). While the Figure 2B caption (line 250) commendably discloses *"against the QRS envelope-demodulated spectrum illustrating Module 3's 0.01–20 Hz passband"*, the main text (Section 3.1, line 257) reads as though Figure 2B is a direct 0.01–20 Hz linear filter recording, and the legend inside Figure 2B says `"Passband 0.01–20 Hz (Mod 3: Fundamental + Harmonics)"`.
* **Concrete Solution:**
  1. In **Section 3.1** (lines 254–262), briefly note (a) that pinching `AF7` and `FPz` off-head leaves the `TP10` driven right-leg (`DRL`) sensor floating (so touching `TP10` with a right-hand finger or relying on the $20\text{ Hz}$ low-pass filter is required to suppress $60\text{ Hz}$ common-mode noise), (b) that Figure 2B reconstructs the sub-2 Hz fundamental from the Module 2 trace via full-wave QRS envelope demodulation (and suggest that adding a one-line `Math.abs(x)` full-wave rectification step or window mean-removal/Hann tapering prior to `fft` in `EEGEduHeartSpectra` is a natural software upgrade to prevent low-frequency drift and harmonic hopping), and (c) that both low-frequency rectangular zero-padding leakage ($0.5\text{--}0.75\text{ Hz}$) and student harmonic-halving (`/2`) contribute to the $\sim 21\text{ BPM}$ downward shift in Module 3 absolute means while canceling out in the within-subject posture contrast.
  2. In **Section 2.2** (Equation 1, line 208), clarify that when $N_{\text{win}} < N_{\text{FFT}}$ (zero-padding in Module 3, $2048 < 8192$), `@neurosity/pipes` appends $N_{\text{FFT}} - N_{\text{win}}$ zeros and normalizes by $2 / N_{\text{FFT}}$, scaling linear amplitudes by $N_{\text{win}} / N_{\text{FFT}} = 0.25$.

### 3. Module 12 (`EEGEduPredict`) In-Browser KNN Architecture vs. Cross-Subject Validation (`StandardScaler` vs. Unit-$L_2$ Normalization and Effective FFT Aperture)
* **Files & Lines:** `paper1_eegedu.tex` (Section 2.3, line 243; Section 3.4, line 343; Figure 5C caption, line 332), `generate_figures.py` (lines 638–669, 735–767), `stats_macros.tex` (lines 181–190), and `repos/EEGEdu/src/components/PageSwitcher/components/EEGEduPredict/EEGEduPredict.js` (lines 29–41, 165–213).
* **Technical Issue:**  
  There are three important details connecting the live browser implementation in `EEGEduPredict.js` to the cross-subject benchmark in Section 3.4 and Figure 5C:
  1. **Unit-$L_2$ Normalization vs. `StandardScaler` in Text and Figure 5C:** In `paper1_eegedu.tex` (lines 243 and 343), the authors accurately explain that `ml5.KNNClassifier` normalizes each input feature vector to unit Euclidean length ($\tilde{\mathbf{x}} = \mathbf{x} / \|\mathbf{x}\|_2$, so Euclidean distance is monotonically equivalent to cosine similarity). In `generate_figures.py` (lines 654–668) and `stats_macros.tex` (lines 185–190), the authors actually computed the exact `Normalizer(norm="l2")` 5-fold `GroupKFold` cross-validation benchmarks (`\BCIKNNLTwoFullAcc` $= 67.9\%$, $\text{AUC} = 0.72$; `\BCIKNNLTwoAlphaAcc` $= 76.9\%$, $\text{AUC} = 0.78$), which **outperform** the `StandardScaler` pipeline (`\BCIKNNFullAcc` $= 67.2\%$, $\text{AUC} = 0.72$; `\BCIKNNAlphaAcc` $= 72.4\%$, $\text{AUC} = 0.79$)! However, `paper1_eegedu.tex` (line 343) and Figure 5C (`generate_figures.py` lines 738–739) only report the `StandardScaler` numbers ($67.2\%$ and $72.4\%$) while leaving the `\BCIKNNLTwo*` macros unused in the text!
  2. **Effective FFT Aperture in Module 12 (`duration: 512` vs. `bins: 256`):** In `EEGEduPredict.js` (lines 33–38), the settings are `duration: 512` (2.0 s), `interval: 256` (1.0 s), and `bins: 256`. Because `@neurosity/pipes` `fft({ bins: 256 })` slices the trailing `bins` samples whenever `safeSamples.length > bins` (`safeSamples.slice(safeSamples.length - 256)`), the FFT in Module 12 actually transforms the **trailing 256 samples (1.0 s aperture)** stepped every 256 samples (1.0 s, 0% overlap), not a 2.0 s FFT! In Section 2.2 (line 216), the authors carefully explained this trailing-256-sample slice for Modules 5, 6, 9, and 10, so they should include Module 12 there as well and update line 243 (*"trains a live $k$-nearest neighbors classifier on 1~s trailing left-temporal spectral vectors emitted every 1~s from 2~s filtered buffers"*).
  3. **Three-Class Live Paradigm (Eyes Closed, Eyes Open, and Blinking):** In `EEGEduPredict.js` (lines 195–211), the live browser UI trains a **3-class** classifier on `TP9` (`data.psd[0]`): Class A (`Eyes Closed`), Class B (`Eyes Open`), and Class C (`Blinking`), and `window.enoughLabels` requires at least one example in all three classes (`numLabels === 3`, line 171) before enabling live prediction. Highlighting that Class C uses intentional eye blinks—which project with massive $\sim 205\ \mu\text{V}$ low-frequency energy onto `TP9` via the `FPz` reference dipole established in Module 4—provides a fantastic conceptual link across the curriculum.
* **Concrete Solution:**
  1. In **Section 3.4** (line 343) and the **Figure 5C caption** (line 332), report the unit-$L_2$ normalized `GroupKFold` accuracy alongside the $z$-scored accuracy (e.g., unit-$L_2$ normalization matching `ml5.KNNClassifier`: full 60-bin $\text{Accuracy} = \BCIKNNLTwoFullAcc\%, \text{AUC} = \BCIKNNLTwoFullAUC$; alpha-prominence $\text{Accuracy} = \BCIKNNLTwoAlphaAcc\%, \text{AUC} = \BCIKNNLTwoAlphaAUC$).
  2. In **Section 2.2** (line 216) and **Section 2.3** (line 243), clarify that Module 12's `duration: 512, interval: 256, bins: 256` configuration transforms the trailing 256 samples (1.0 s aperture at 1 Hz bin resolution, updated every 1.0 s) on `TP9` across three user-calibrated states (`Eyes Closed`, `Eyes Open`, and `Blinking`).

### 4. Reconciling Locked vs. Exposed Parameter Sliders in Module 11 (`EEGEduEvoked`) and Module 11 CSV Serialization Detail
* **Files & Lines:** `paper1_eegedu.tex` (Section 2.3, line 219 vs. Section 3.4, line 341), `repos/EEGEdu/src/components/PageSwitcher/PageSwitcher.js` (lines 344–345, 374–379), and `repos/EEGEdu/src/components/PageSwitcher/components/EEGEduEvoked/EEGEduEvoked.js` (lines 115–178, 284, 312).
* **Technical Issue:**  
  1. **Slider Visibility Contradiction:** In `paper1_eegedu.tex` line 219, the text accurately states that interactive signal-processing sliders are *"exposed in Modules 4--10; locked in Modules 1--3 and 11--12 to preserve standardized benchmark timing"* (indeed, in `PageSwitcher.js` lines 340–379, `pipeSettingsDisplay()` returns `null` for `intro`, `heartRaw`, `heartSpectra`, and `evoked`, and `funPredict.renderSliders` returns `null`). However, in **Section 3.4** (line 341), the text states for Module 11: *"second, lowering the high-pass filter slider from 2.0~Hz down to 0.1~Hz prevents causal phase distortion of slow late potentials"*. Because `PageSwitcher.js` line 375 returns `null` for `case evoked:`, the high-pass filter slider defined in `EEGEduEvoked.js` (lines 115–178) is not rendered in the live UI unless the user either explores filter cutoffs in Module 4 (`EEGEduRaw`, where the default low cutoff is $0.1\text{ Hz}$) or enables `funEvoked.renderSliders` in `PageSwitcher.js`!
  2. **CSV Serialization in `EEGEduEvoked.js` (line 312):** In `EEGEduEvoked.js` line 312, `saveToCSV` writes each 1-sample epoch using `Object.values(x).join(",")`. Because `@neurosity/pipes` `epoch` emits `x = { data: [[ch0], [ch1], [ch2], [ch3]], info: { samplingRate: 256, startTime: ... } }`, `Object.values(x).join(",")` outputs the 4 (or 5) channel voltages followed by `[object Object]` under the trailing `"info"` column header (line 284). While spreadsheets still parse the first 8 columns (`Timestamp`, `Marker`, `SpaceBar`, `TouchMarker`, `ch0`–`ch3`) cleanly, noting this or using `Object.values(x.data).join(",")` in future releases is good hygiene.
* **Concrete Solution:**  
  In **Section 3.4** (line 341), revise the wording from *"lowering the high-pass filter slider from 2.0~Hz down to 0.1~Hz"* to clarify that whereas Module 11 locks its default bandpass to $2\text{--}20\text{ Hz}$ in the standard UI to suppress slow drift during brief classroom recordings, instructors use the interactive cutoff slider in Module 4 ($0.1\text{--}100\text{ Hz}$) or unlock `renderSliders` in `EEGEduEvoked.js` to demonstrate how lowering the high-pass cutoff from $2.0\text{ Hz}$ to $0.1\text{ Hz}$ avoids causal IIR phase distortion of slow late potentials (P300).

### 5. Multi-Headset BLE Coexistence in Unshielded Classrooms and Dry-Electrode Contact Mechanics
* **Files & Lines:** `paper1_eegedu.tex` (Section 2.1, lines 182–183; Section 2.3, line 221; Section 3.2, lines 271–275).
* **Technical Issue:**  
  Operating 15 to 25 Muse headbands simultaneously in the 2.4 GHz ISM band inside a university lecture hall full of student Wi-Fi and Bluetooth devices is a non-trivial RF engineering feat. In Section 2.1 (line 182), the authors describe two practical procedures that made this work: labeling each headband with its four-character hexadecimal factory MAC suffix (`Muse-XXXX`) and pairing in staggered 30-second rows. From a mobile EEG methodology standpoint, two brief additions will help other laboratories replicate their high valid-data completion rates ($89.3\%\text{--}100\%$ in Modules 2–3; $77\text{--}78/86$ per condition sheet in Module 9):
  1. **2.4 GHz BLE Adaptive Frequency Hopping vs. Discovery Collisions:** Once a BLE GATT connection is established across the 37 data channels, adaptive frequency hopping handles 20+ concurrent 256 Hz streams (each requiring only $\sim 4\text{ kbps}$ of payload bandwidth) with minimal packet loss; the primary bottleneck in classroom settings is **advertising channel congestion** on the 3 BLE advertising channels (Channels 37, 38, 39) during simultaneous discovery and pairing, which is why staggered row-by-row connection is effective.
  2. **Mechanical Tension & Dry-Electrode Polarization at `TP9`/`TP10`:** Unlike the flat forehead sensors (`AF7`/`AF8`), the conductive silicone ear tabs (`TP9`/`TP10`) sit across curved mastoid skin behind the pinna where eyeglasses, hair, or jaw tension easily break skin-to-electrode coupling. When a dry mastoid sensor momentarily loses contact, the high input impedance picks up large $60\text{ Hz}$ mains interference and slow half-cell recharge transients. In Module 1 (`EEGEduIntro`), the $2\text{--}20\text{ Hz}$ bandpass filter intentionally excludes $60\text{ Hz}$ line noise so that the rolling standard deviation threshold ($\text{SD} < 15\text{--}25\ \mu\text{V}$) specifically indexes low-frequency mechanical/contact stability before students inspect broadband $0.1\text{--}100\text{ Hz}$ traces (including $60\text{ Hz}$ mains) in Module 4.
* **Concrete Solution:**  
  Add 2–3 sentences in **Section 2.1** or **Section 2.3 (Module 1)** noting why staggered pairing prevents collisions on the three BLE advertising channels whereas established GATT streams occupy only $\sim 4\text{ kbps}$ per headset across 37 hopping data channels, and how Module 1's $2\text{--}20\text{ Hz}$ passband isolates mechanical contact stability from $60\text{ Hz}$ line noise.
